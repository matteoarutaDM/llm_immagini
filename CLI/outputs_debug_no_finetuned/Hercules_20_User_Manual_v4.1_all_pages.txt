• Provide technical information about the aircraft, its accessories, and its remote-control system. 
• Provide the HERCULES 20 operation procedures and maintenance procedures.

• Serve as a refresher guide – following a long hiatus in aircraft operations.

operation of the HERCULES 20.

required, DRONE VOLT offers single-day, or more, refresher training packages.

1.5 Operating conditions 
Reminder: Current regulations do not allow flying over people or gatherings of people unless specifically 
authorized by the competent authority , either national Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) or Air Navigation 
Service Provider (ANSP). 
DRONE VOLT aircraft have been designed, engineered and manufactured to be compliant with the regulatory 
frameworks in all the jurisdictions they are sold in. It is therefore implied that the O WNER/OPERATOR must 
follow and respect the laws and rules of the country in which they fly , as well as have the necessary 
authorizations for the flight mission. Compliance of a ll planning and flight operations is the sole 
responsibility of the O WNER/OPERATORS, considering they will be held responsible for any wrongdoing or 
damages caused to other aircraft, or to people and property on the ground. 
Before any mission, good practice demands that the crew appropriately mark out the aircraft’s projected 
operating area on the ground (third party exclusion zone) and constantly control the access to this area 
before, during, and after the flight operations. This is to avoid potential accidents, but also to prevent any 
damage to the aircraft. 
The operator and flight crew should, in conjunction with local regulations, determine and enforce the safe 
distances required between pilot/crew and the drone, and the safe distance to be maintained between the 
drone and any third-party: 
• Typically, the pilot should not be within 10 meters of the aircraft when the propellers are (or will 
start) spinning. 
• Also, any other person not involved in the operation of the drone should typically stand at least 30 
meters away from the aircraft when it takes off or lands. 
• Proper safety perimeters should be established according to national regulations. 
 
The HERCULES 20 has been designed to fly under visual line -of-sight (VLOS) protocols and under visual 
meteorological conditions (VMC). DRONE VOLT will never approve, allow or condone operating the HERCULES 
20 in beyond visual line-of-sight mission scenarios. 
Before each flight, the OWNER/OPERATOR’s crew must ensure the proper functioning of the aircraft and 
should be aware of the local weather conditions, at takeoff time but also for the duration of the mission 
and beyond. The atmospheric conditions should be constantly monitored, in case of sudden changes or 
discrepancies with the weather forecast. Flight operations must be halted or cancelled when any observed 
weather conditions have the potential to infringe on safe flight parameters. 
The HERCULES 20 is not designed to fly in heavy rain, however moderate rain is accepted. Nor is it designed 
to fly in strong winds (refer to the Technical Specification of the aircraft). Please check the local weather 
forecast and wind estimation near the flight area. Also note that the wind is usually stronger at high 
altitude; wind velocity at ground level is not always a good indication of wind conditions 100 meters above 
ground. 
Please also be aware of the Kp index (geomagnetic disruption forecast) and act accordingly. Many 
applications are available to monitor this crucial parameter.

Do not fly when the minimal technical conditions are not met (weak satellite reception, electromagnetic 
disturbances, weak batteries, unstable magnetic compass, insufficient maintenance, and general condition 
of the device, etc.). The OWNER/OPERATOR’s pilot is ultimately responsible for flight safety and for the 
decision to fly regardless of any third-party pressure. 
Keep a safe distance from any telecom communication towers and antennas, as well as high-voltage power 
lines. They could degrade the reception of satellite signals, affect the on- board magnetic compass and 
cause electromagnetic interference with the command link. The proximity of these hazards could result in 
poor positioning of the aircraft in the flight space, failure to adhere to a virtual barrier (geofencing) and a 
reduction of stability, which could result in a crash. 
The proximity of large metallic objects and structures that can cause electromagnetic interference, which 
in turn can affect on-board compass readings and drone performance, should be avoided. 
Furthermore, tall buildings may pose a threat: their reflective surface may disrupt satellite signals and thus 
disrupt the GNSS positioning of the aircraft. Also, working between tall buildings can abruptly cut the GNSS 
positioning as well , when satellites moving in the sky above are suddenly occult ed, passing behind tall 
structures. Induced turbulence due to wind swirling around the buildings can slightly disturb the barometer 
and locally degrade altitude hold and stabilization. It is therefore recommended not to approach less than 
2 meters from the surface of any building. 
Knowledge of any concurrent activity in the vicinity (aerial or on the ground) is an important parameter to 
account for when carrying out a drone operation. Consult the locally applicable NOTAMs , and any other 
relevant documents, apps or websites to properly assess the safety of the area . Validate this information 
by performing a thorough site survey to identify any potentially conflicting activities or safety hazards.

2.4.1 Installing the battery in the HERCULES 20 
The HERCULES 20 is operated with four batteries matched in two pairs: left and right sides of the aircraft. 
The HERCULES 20 will not start nor fly if just a single pair of batteries is installed inside the main body cavity; 
Likewise, if only the sidepod trays are used. While the drone will power up with a single set installed (a 
battery in one of the two internal bays and another battery in the same -side sidepod tray), this 
configuration should only be used on the ground for setup and testing as it would severely affect stability 
in flight. 
To ensure the stability and proper balance of the Hercules 20, always take off with four batteries onboard. 
To avoid sparks while connecting, is recommended to hook up the black connectors before the red ones. 
The procedure for installing the batteries into the HERCULES 20 aircraft is as follows: 
1. The HERELINK remote controller must be turned on prior to connecting the aircraft batteries. (See 
Section3) 
2. Check the health status of each battery with the battery tester supplied with the HERCULES 20. 
a. Check each battery’s voltage and ensure that it is equivalent for all batteries. 
b. Look for anomalies by checking individual cell voltages in each battery. 
c. The battery-checker displays the following information when a battery is connected to it: 
3. Attach two batteries to the external battery trays: fit the black connector first, then the red one. 
4. Insert the two internal batteries and attach only the black connector(s). 
5. When ready to power on the aircraft , connect the remaining red connectors in the internal battery 
compartment. When this is done, an LED status indicator will light up and the built -in beeper will 
sound to signal the start of the initialization phase of the aircraft.

2.6 Battery handling and storage instructions 
Lithium polymer batteries should always be considered dangerous items. Care should be given to their 
handling: 
• LiPo batteries are flammable and sensitive to shocks. 
• Store and transport with care, in dry, temperate conditions. 
• The voltage read at the end of a flight goes up by ≈2V once the drone has landed. 
• It considered is normal when batteries heat up moderately in flight. When a single cell -or the whole battery- 
is unusually hot at the end of a flight, that battery needs to be discarded. 
• In cold weather, the range may be greatly reduced. To minimize this the batteries should be kept at a 
moderate temperature before being installed on the aircraft shortly before flight. 
 BELOW 3.6 V PER CELL (43 V), THE VOLTAGE WILL DROP VERY QUICKLY 
• No sharp object should be allowed to touch the batteries at any time. 
• Do not store LiPo batteries fully charged for more than 20 days, as they may swell. For long term storage, 
batteries should be discharged to 3.7 V per cell and stored in a strong fireproof case. This will significantly 
increase the batteries’ lifespan. 
• Charge at the recommended amperage of 16A or less. 
• Charging should be performed with the batteries put inside a "LiPo bag", a fire-retardant pouch, which allows 
the user to move a potentially problematic /failing battery without touching it. This bag will allow enough 
time to carry the batteries to a safe location should they fail while charging. 
• While charging, LiPo batteries must be monitored at all times. 
 NEVER LEAVE AN OPERATING CHARGER UNATTENDED! 
• Never attempt to recharge or use a damaged, swollen or dented battery. 
A failing battery will usually show gradual signs of deterioration, long before any catastrophic occurrence. 
It will start taking longer to charge, it will likely not match the charging rate, nor time, of its partner 
(batteries should always be charged in pairs). Using the battery checker will reveal significant differences 
in cell voltage and resistance within the battery. If these signs go unnoticed, or worse, if the battery has 
suffered piercing damage, it can fail catastrophically, this usually occurs immediately on damaging the 
battery or during the charge cycle. The manner of failure will depend on the current state of charge of the 
battery, becoming more dramatic with increasing levels of battery charge. A catastrophic failure will usually 
follow this pattern: 
• A cell within the battery will begin to swell 
• It will emit a hissing sound and start releasing toxic smoke with a pungent smell. 
• The cell will eventually rupture, release more toxic smoke, and then ignite loudly. 
• When a first cell ignites, the other cells will shortly follow in cascad ing order along the same catastrophic 
failure pattern. Do not approach a battery that has begun this process; it is not safe until all cells have burned 
through or have been extinguished.

3.8.4 Failsafe systems 
As standard the HERCULES 20 employs two automatic failsafe actions and a manually triggered kill switch. 
1. RETURN TO LAUNCH (RTL): triggered by C2 link loss or HOME/RTL command from the HERELINK 
controller. This will cause the aircraft to travel vertically to the preset RTL altitude (configurable 
in the SAFETY tab) and then return to hover briefly over the launch location before descending 
to land. 
2. LAND: triggered by critically low battery. This will cause the aircraft to descend slowly (up to 
3m/s) while ROLL, PITCH and YAW remain under the operator’s control during descent. 
3. Kill: triggered by the kill switch on the HERELINK controller (long press of "D" button). This serves 
as an emergency stop: It will halt all the motors’ rotation regardless of current state. 
 WARNING: USING THE KILL SWITCH IN FLIGHT WILL CAUSE THE AIRCRAFT TO FALL! 
PLEASE BE AWARE OF CURRENT MOMENTUM, WIND, POTENTIAL TRAJECTORY AND 
LIKELY IMPACT ZONE BEFORE ACTIVATING.

3.8.7 Safety setup tab 
The SAFETY tab is used to define battery alarm thresholds and the actions to be taken with each alarm level. 
 
This tab can also be used to set the FAILSAFE actions (i.e. actions in the event of critical battery or loss of 
radio signal), the GEOFENCE (the parameters limiting the flight zone) and the RTL (return to launch) altitude 
values. 
Low voltage thresholds for the battery alarms need to be initially configured: 
 At 43 V (low voltage threshold) the drone engages RTL mode. 
For obvious safety reasons, it is highly recommended to initiate a controlled landing procedure before the 
activation of the 43 V alarm. In some specific conditions or flight environment, the operator may choose 
to disable the RTL action linked to this alarm by selecting NONE, for instance. The alarm beeps will still be 
audible, and it will be visible on the R adio Controller. However, the drone will continue to fly normally , 
therefore transferring the responsibility to the pilot to swiftly initiate the appropriate measures to ensure 
the safety of the operation. 
 At 41 V (critical voltage threshold) the drone engages a LAND procedure. 
Failsafe Triggers 
The "GENERAL FAILSAFE TRIGGERS" section allows selection of the actions to be undertaken automatically by 
the airborne drone when the C2 link radio signal is lost. 
The usual default setting for signal loss is RTL. In some very specific cases (automatic missions outside of 
radio range, small or crowded airspace, etc.) this setting can be changed to either LAND or CONTINUE MISSION 
(this setting is not recommended for standard flight operations).

Return to launch 
The RETURN TO LAUNCH (RTL) settings give the user the possibility to set different scenarios for the RTL action.

In specific conditions, it may be advisable to increase the RTL altitude to avoid obstacles, such as structures 
or trees, that would be in the way when the aircraft automatically returns to its takeoff point. There is a 
caveat to this approach however: setting the RTL altitude too high may be counterproductive since climbing 
will deplete the batteries further and potentially prevent the aircraft completing the trip back to the 
launchpad.

3.8.11 H erelink Satellite Reception Icon 
 
 
This icon indicates the number of satellites from which the HERELINK is currently receiving signal. 
For instance, in the image above, the number of satellites the HERELINK is receiving is "0". In such a case, it 
is likely that the drone is indoors, in a location where there is no satellite coverage, or the antenna 
connection is faulty. This situation can also occur when the aircraft is surrounded by large obstacles or tall 
buildings nearby, or if it’s set to operate in very poor meteorological conditions. 
To maintain proper GNSS positioning, the minimum number of satellites required to fly safely is 6. But, to 
fly the drone in optimal conditions, this value shall at least be 10. 
The number of satellites notwithstanding, the second number, the horizontal dilution of precision (or 
HDOP), is the most important parameter to help assess the robustness of the GNSS coverage and ensure 
the safety of a flight. Again, in the image above, we see a value of "100" for the HDOP. This number’s value 
should be less than 1 to fly safely. The closer this value is to 1, the more likely it is to get inaccurate compass 
readings. Hence, in this example, it would not be safe to fly the HERCULES 20; The operator would have to 
wait until the minimum numbers of satellite is attained and HDOP is in the safe range.

As stipulated in Section 1.5 - Operating conditions of this Guide, in order to operate the HERCULES 20, remote

certification and securing the access to the intended operation ar ea. Furthermore, t hey must also be 
knowledgeable about the laws, regulations and bylaws from any other relevant government agency 
regulating aircraft use, airspace access and any other aspect of drone operation that could infringe on 
existing rules such as: 
 Civil responsibility and negligent behavior offences. 
 Radio frequency spectrum allocation, power regulations and usage. 
 Handling and transportation of dangerous goods. 
 Rights of land, trespass rules and privacy laws. 
 Relevant dispositions of the applicable criminal code. 
 Territorial limitations, borders control, customs & immigration, and national security laws. 
 Applicable occupational health & safety regulations for the operation team, but also for bystanders, 
as well as product safety legislation that may apply. 
The OWNER/OPERATOR’s pilot and crew need to be capable of operating the system, which implies that their 
knowledge, their training and flight experience must be current . It is the responsibility of the 
OWNER/OPERATOR to ensure their operational personnel’s skills and knowledge are current. 
The crew, and especially the pilot , must be fit to operate a remotely piloted aircraft such as the H ERCULES 
20. The IMSAFEE framework provides valuable guidelines to evaluate the fitness of flight personnel. It 
stands for: 
 I – Illness  Any symptoms of a health condition? 
 M – Medication  Any side effects of a medication (or lack thereof)? 
 S – Stress  Any stress induced by job, finances, health or family issues, etc. ? 
 A – Alcohol  Any alcohol consumption in the last 12 hours? Hangover symptoms? 
 F – Fatigue  Not enough or poor sleep? Jet lagged? Overworked? 
 E – Emotion  Anxiety? Sadness or anger? Any condition that may affect judgement? 
 E – Eating  Any food-related issue? Digestion and proper hydration?

The IMSAFEE framework is used as a self-check tool to ensure that the crew is as ready to fly as the drone

potential hazards, and then finding effective mitigation strategies. At all times, the crew’s health and well-

4.5.2 Operation planning procedures 
The OWNER/OPERATORS must define concepts of operations (CONOPs) that are compliant with their national 
and local regulations and bylaws. After the airspace type has been determined and the operation has been 
deemed possible and legit, the land and airspace access procedures must be m ade so all required 
authorizations and supporting documents must be obtained or delivered before the scheduled date of the 
operation. 
The scope of the operation must be defined taking in account required safety perimeter, and its 
configuration at the specific operation site considered , to establish the safe distances required between 
pilot/crew and the drone, and the safe distance to be maintained between the drone and any third- party 
according to national regulations and CONOP. The operation site’s specific and critical characteristics must 
be identified and analyzed: obstacles, GNSS coverage constraints, wind direction, sun orientation and 
location at the time of the operation, the possible presence of aircraft in the airspace above, activities on 
the ground in the vicinity, or any other types of safety hazards. This analysis will help determine the safety 
measures to be deployed, such as the use (or not) and parameters of a geofence. 
The weather forecast for the planned day, time and specific location of the operation must be checked: not 
only the temperature and precipitations, but also wind velocity, and direction. Forecasts for atmospheric 
visibility should also be checked, as well as the Kp index since geomagnetic disturbances could have a direct 
impact on the operation’s safety. Consult the locally applicable NOTAMs, and any other relevant 
documents, apps or websites to properly assess the safety conditions of the area at the specific time of the 
operation. 
When the planning phase is complete, all the selected equipment must be gathered, inspected and tested. 
All the batteries must be checked and fully charged. Not only the drone’s batteries, but also any battery 
used to power the other components of the system. Recharge or replace any battery to ensure every piece 
of equipment can perform flawlessly. 
At this stage, perform the first section of the off -site checklist ( Section 4.5.3) : “Before packing the 
equipment”. 
Then, put all the components in their respective protective cases and verify they are properly located and 
secured in their allocated space. Check that nothing is loose. Any loose piece of equipment could be 
damaged, or cause damage to other equipment during transport. Also verify the structural integrity of all 
transport cases and take some pictures if a third-party transporter is to be involved. In such a case, also put 
all the relevant transport labels on the cases , lock them and then, if necessary, apply a seal (using a piece 
of adhesive tape bridging the joint between the lid and body of the case) to detect possible tampering with 
the equipment during transport. 
When everything is ready to ship, t he second section of the off -site checklist ( Section 4.5.3) can be 
performed. 
Load the equipment in the transport vehicle… and check that nothing has been left behind

4.5.4 On-site preparation procedures 
Operation feasibility 
Before deploying the crew on the site, ensure that all the paperwork is in order and that all required 
authorizations. Also check that all the concerned services and agencies have been duly notified. 
Survey the operation site to confirm the situation there does not reveal any problematic elements that may 
have been missed at the planning stage: 
• Obstacles in the flight zone such as trees, lamp posts or suspended cables. 
• Possible sources of interference: cellular antenna, radar, or large metallic objects and structures 
• The configuration of surrounding buildings that could bounce or disrupt GNSS signal. 
• Human activities in the vicinity such as a gathering of people or a construction zone. 
Assess the weather and verify there are no discrepancies with the forecasted atmospheric conditions. If 
unexpected and sudden changes in atmospheric conditions are observed, the f light operations must be 
postponed or cancelled. Bad weather conditions can compromise flight safety or impair the quality of the 
expected outcome of the operation. 
In short, make sure the planned operation is technically feasible at that time on this site. 
 WARNING: THE OWNER/OPERATOR’S PILOT IS RESPONSIBLE FOR FLIGHT SAFETY AND

Site configuration

parties. If required or planned, i nstall the crowd control apparatus aimed at limiting access around the

6.4 H IGH-DRA payload 
The HIGH-DRA variant of the HERCULES 20 aircraft is fitted with mounting points on the underside of the main 
chassis for attaching the lance of a high-pressure fluid spraying system. 
 
 
 
With appropriate selection of pump, hose, spraying lance and nozzle, the ensemble presents a stable, 
accurate and highly maneuverable aerial spraying system for multiple spray-based use cases. 
 
 
 
6.4.1 High- Dra payload operational concerns 
Operating a UAV with a high- pressure fluid spraying system attached brings additional concerns for the 
flight crew. These are summarized below along with an additional safety benefit associated with most high-
pressure fluid pumps. 
1. Reaction thrust. When high pressure fluid is projected from the aircraft, consideration must be given to 
the resultant forces exerted in flight. Directing the fluid vertically (up/down) when output from the lance 
nozzle can hinder and limit aircraft maneuverability. The nozzle should be as close to horizontal as possible 
when the lance is extended out from the aircraft body. If the operator wishes to direct water vertically 
downward the lance nozzle should be as close to the aircraft center-of-mass as possible, thus, minimizing 
the lever arm from reaction forces which could cause the aircraft to pitch. The aircraft will automatically 
manage horizontal components of the resultant force induced by the thrust produced by water jetting out 
horizontally. E ven at 3500 psi, t he impact of this recoil may only alter the drone’s position by a few 
centimeters.

---------------------------------------- [FINE PAGINA 82] ----------------------------------------

Hercules 20 – User Guide 
 
82 
 © 2025 DRONE VOLT – All rights reserved 
2. Hose management. The high-pressure hose should be managed during flight; it is important to reduce any 
tugs and lateral forces on the aircraft from the hose. Please ensure there is sufficient unspooled, untangled 
hose arranged below the intended work area available for e asy uptake as the aircraft moves. When 
landing, ensure no hose buckles under the aircraft, hence preventing proper level contact with the ground; 
a kinked hose caught under the landing-gear could cause a pitch or roll bringing spinning propellors into 
contact with the ground. Similarly, a kinked hose could interfere with the propellors. 
3. Additional mass of the lance, hose and water. The total suspended mass is slightly offset by the high -
pressure water flow and the ≈ 90° deflection before entering the lance. However, for the purpose of safety 
risk mitigation, it is necessary to assume the full mass of lance, nozzle, hose -aloft and water -aloft are 
carried by the aircraft as payload. For reference, the mass of one meter of ¼" [≈6mm] bore high-pressure 
fiber-sheathed hose filled with water is ≈133g. Consequently, in the imperial system, a foot of this same 
water filled hose will weigh ≈ 1.42oz (0.09lb). 
4. Moisture build-up. Depending on the weather and choice of nozzle, fluid may gather on external surfaces 
of the aircraft during normal operation. Please inspect and evaluate the aircraft during each power-cycle. 
Mop up and dry any locations where fluid could conceivably penetrate and threaten aircraft functionality. 
Note the H ERCULES 20 has an IP54 rating and thus is considered protected from water splashes from all 
directions including the use of a water spray without shield. 
 
 
 
5. Safe working distance. It is acknowledged that flying close to surfaces is an unavoidable hazard when 
operating an aerial sprayer UAV system. However, despite a dual GNSS configuration that is increasing the 
stability of the aircraft avionics and thrust system, the pilot must remain vigilant for turbulent air streams 
around building edges, or vortexing over roofs, and GNSS conflicted/denied areas. To support this, DRONE 
VOLT prescribes minimum safe distances of 2m from the sprayed workface in flight, and a takeoff zone that 
shall be no less than 4m from any building facade. To ensure safe operation and adequate GNSS coverage, 
the designated takeoff area should also allow for at least 50% of the sky to be visible.
